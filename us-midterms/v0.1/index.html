What this is · What we aim to discover

Not another prediction dashboard.

The 2026 midterms offer a way to examine how evidence acquires—and sometimes fails to acquire—authority. We use the familiar 5W1H questions to investigate the record, then distinguish descriptive, predictive, causal and prescriptive analytics. Each requires different methods and permits different conclusions.

Descriptive · 5W1H

Establish the record

Who produced the evidence? What does it measure? Where, when, why was it collected, and how? We trace sources, timestamps and definitions.

Demonstrated: source and timeline audits

Compare only original, pre-outcome forecasts with equivalent certified targets; distinguish qualitative race ratings from calibrated probabilities.

Defend a conclusion

Specify competing explanations and the data needed to test them; then ask whether a publication claim is justified, qualified or should be withheld.

Demonstrated: educational decision receipt, not causal effect

Why original forecast dates matter

A forecast published after partial results are known is not eligible for a pre-election test. An Election Day source with date but no time requires verification. Cook's Nov 2 ratings are ordinal; Silver Bulletin's Nov 3 release is a prospective acquisition lead, not a locally verified frozen probability panel. Read the predefined gates.

What changes in the information?

Unofficial report

Counts may reflect only ballots processed so far. State agencies publish evolving returns. A media race call is a distinct journalistic determination.

Canvass and audit

Eligible outstanding ballots are counted, records reconciled and procedures checked, according to state rules. These changes do not inherently imply an error or irregularity.

Official certification

Appropriate election officials attest to the final result for their jurisdiction. It must be verified for the exact contest before describing it as certified.

Two kinds of uncertainty

Epistemic uncertainty: missing snapshots, inconsistent denominators, and unconfirmed publication times leave knowledge incomplete.

Process uncertainty: the normal counting and certification sequence has not finished. Confusing the two can turn an ordinary update into an incorrect story.

Which evidence is not yet present?

V0.1 has no frozen election-night vote files or certified 2026 House outcome pairs, so it does not plot ballot-count revisions or declare certification complete. See acquisition plan and verified timeline metadata.

What must happen before a U.S. geographic results map or model evaluation?

Download district geometry with the correct 2026 boundary version; obtain state-issued district results with timestamps and later certified snapshots; reconcile district IDs and vote definitions; acquire legal original forecast releases with independent pre-election capture times; then run the predefined validation gates. A choropleth built before these joins would make missing or incorrect data look authoritative.

Forward-looking test

Would the Decision Twin improve decisions?

The current interface does not prove that it does. A future preregistered reader study should compare a conventional results view, guided evidence experience and optional Evidence Lab. Measure accurate identification of evidence status, unsupported causal inferences, responsible abstention, source retrieval effort and confidence calibration—rather than aesthetic preference alone.
